# Catalog Research

These notes document the sources behind Discora’s songwriting, recording, release, and artwork entries. Session numbers follow the source releases. Recollections are attributed to the person who gave them, and fan interpretations are identified where they appear.

## Catalog expansion

The four albums added on November 24, 2026 account for 53 tracks. Titles and durations come from Apple Music’s 2009 digital remasters. Track order and record sides follow the UK releases. Cover images come from the official Beatles catalog, and pressing codes come from the UK discography. ## Original catalog

The November 21, 2026 research covered six albums and 75 tracks. It focused on surviving recordings and session documentation, including the early “Yellow Submarine” demo, the tape loops of “Tomorrow Never Knows,” and the six frames from the *Abbey Road* cover shoot. “How Do You Sleep?” and “I’m the Greatest” document different interactions between former bandmates. [Revolver archive][rev] · [Abbey Road archive][ab] · [Lennon interview, 1971][cashbox] · [Ringo LP credits][ringo]

## Revolver

*The Beatles · 14 tracks*

Apple Corps notes that the cancellation of a third film left the band with four months before its next studio sessions. The first session was for “Tomorrow Never Knows,” which became the closing track. [2022 edition notes][rev]

1. **Taxman.** George Harrison wrote the opening track. Paul McCartney played the guitar solo as well as the bass. [“Taxman” session notes][rev]
2. **Eleanor Rigby.** George Martin arranged the strings for two quartets. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/eleanor-rigby)
3. **I'm Only Sleeping.** A short vibraphone rehearsal survived at the end of a reused tape. Later takes stopped before recording over the whole reel, leaving the fragment that was eventually released. [Anthology rehearsal notes][anth] · [2022 session tracklist][rev]
4. **Love You To.** Harrison placed sitar at the center of an arrangement built around North Indian instruments, including tabla and tambura. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/love-you)
5. **Here, There and Everywhere.** McCartney recalled working out chords by the pool at Lennon’s house while waiting for him to wake up. [McCartney’s account in The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/here-there-and-everywhere)
6. **Yellow Submarine.** On the working tape released in 2022, Lennon sings an early, melancholy verse. The finished version has a much brighter tone and is sung by Ringo Starr. [2022 working tapes and edition notes][rev]
7. **She Said She Said.** Lennon linked the idea for the song to a 1965 conversation with actor Peter Fonda. [Lennon’s account in The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/she-said-she-said)
8. **Good Day Sunshine.** Leonard Bernstein discussed this track on a 1967 CBS television program about pop music. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/good-day-sunshine)
9. **And Your Bird Can Sing.** The 2022 edition includes Take 2 of the first arrangement and Take 5 of the second. The two recordings show how the guitar parts changed. [2022 session tracklist and notes][rev]
10. **For No One.** McCartney recalled asking Alan Civil to play a high F beyond the range George Martin had just described. Civil hit the note. [McCartney’s account and May 19, 1966 session notes](https://www.the-paulmccartney-project.com/session/revolver-session-24/) · [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/no-one)
11. **Doctor Robert.** The band recorded seven takes on April 17 and returned for overdubs two days later. Lennon and McCartney gave different accounts of the doctor’s identity. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/doctor-robert)
12. **I Want to Tell You.** One of Harrison’s working titles was Laxton’s Superb, the name of an apple variety. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/i-want-tell-you)
13. **Got to Get You into My Life.** The album version features a prominent brass section. Take 5 captures an earlier arrangement. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/got-get-you-my-life) · [2022 session tracklist][rev]
14. **Tomorrow Never Knows.** Abbey Road Studios attributes the seagull-like sound to a recording of McCartney laughing. The final arrangement layers several tape loops. [Abbey Road Studios](https://www.abbeyroad.com/news/the-genius-of-paul-mccartney-as-told-by-abbey-roads-cameron-colbeck-2707) · [2022 edition notes][rev]

## Abbey Road

*The Beatles · 17 tracks*

The album had the working title *Everest*. After the idea of a photo in Nepal was dropped, McCartney proposed shooting outside the studio. Iain Macmillan took six photographs from a ladder on August 8, 1969, while a police officer held up traffic. The fifth frame became the cover. [Abbey Road Studios photographs and sketch][studio] · [The Beatles archive][ab]

1. **Come Together.** Timothy Leary asked Lennon to write a song for his California gubernatorial campaign. The Beatles’ recording was later released as a double A-side single with “Something.” [Abbey Road Studios on the song’s origins](https://www.abbeyroad.com/news/the-abbey-road-wall-celebrates-come-together-2598) · [Single release notes](https://www.thebeatles.com/come-together)
2. **Something.** Harrison recorded an electric guitar demo on his 26th birthday. The 2019 edition includes the demo and a strings-only mix. [Anthology demo notes][anth] · [2019 session tracklist][ab19]
3. **Maxwell's Silver Hammer.** Lennon criticized McCartney’s song during a September 1969 band meeting, according to a report on the recording shared by historian Mark Lewisohn. [The Guardian’s report on the meeting tape](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2019/sep/11/the-beatles-break-up-mark-lewisohn-abbey-road-hornsey-road)
4. **Oh! Darling.** The working title was longer. The 2019 edition includes Take 4 for comparison with McCartney’s performance on the album. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/oh-darling) · [2019 session tracklist][ab19]
5. **Octopus's Garden.** Starr wrote the song with help from Harrison during its development. The published writing credit is Starr’s. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/octopuss-garden)
6. **I Want You (She's So Heavy).** The repeating riff ends abruptly. The 2019 edition also includes a version from the Trident Studios sessions. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/i-want-you) · [2019 session tracklist][ab19]
7. **Here Comes the Sun.** Harrison recalled writing the song in Eric Clapton’s garden with one of Clapton’s acoustic guitars after taking a day away from Apple’s offices. [Harrison’s account in The Beatles Anthology, p. 339, cited in UC research](https://escholarship.org/content/qt15t9f8kr/qt15t9f8kr_noSplash_dbacb473bbd6e6a0e08164cb8951e027.pdf)
8. **Because.** Lennon, McCartney, and Harrison recorded three vocal passes each, creating the nine-part vocal arrangement. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/because)
9. **You Never Give Me Your Money.** McCartney wrote the song amid the band’s business disputes around the time Allen Klein joined their management. Its music returns later in the medley. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/you-never-give-me-your-money) · [“Carry That Weight”](https://www.thebeatles.com/carry-weight)
10. **Sun King.** The closing passage plays with words and sounds from several languages. On the session tape, Take 20 flows directly into “Mean Mr. Mustard.” [Official “Sun King” recording](https://www.thebeatles.com/sun-king) · [2019 session tracklist][ab19]
11. **Mean Mr. Mustard.** A July 1969 trial edit put “Her Majesty” after this track. The final album moves directly into “Polythene Pam.” [Trial edit and album sequence][ab19]
12. **Polythene Pam.** The guitar and drums lead straight into “She Came In Through the Bathroom Window.” The session release includes Take 27 of both pieces. [2019 session tracklist][ab19]
13. **She Came In Through The Bathroom Window.** The Beatles played this song during the Get Back sessions before recording it for Abbey Road. A January rehearsal appears on the Let It Be archive release. [Get Back sessions](https://www.thebeatles.com/let-it-be-special-anniversary-editions-2021) · [Abbey Road session tracklist][ab19]
14. **Golden Slumbers.** McCartney recalled finding the words of an old lullaby in sheet music at his father’s house. He wrote his own melody because he could not read the notation. [McCartney’s account in The Lyrics and Anthology](https://www.the-paulmccartney-project.com/song/golden-slumbers/)
15. **Carry That Weight.** All four Beatles sing on the track. Music from “You Never Give Me Your Money” returns in the middle and ties the medley together. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/carry-weight)
16. **The End.** Starr plays a drum solo, followed by alternating guitar solos from McCartney, Harrison, and Lennon. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/end)
17. **Her Majesty.** The short track appeared inside the medley on a trial edit. On the album it follows “The End” after a pause. [The Beatles archive](https://www.thebeatles.com/her-majesty) · [July 30, 1969 trial edit][ab19]

## Imagine

*John Lennon · 10 tracks*

Many sessions took place at Lennon and Ono’s home studio at Tittenhurst Park. The archive team notes that microphones picked up household sounds audible in some raw mixes. Some string overdubs were recorded later in New York. [Engineer recollections and raw mixes][raw] · [Album credits](https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/imagine/)

1. **Imagine.** Lennon connected the song to Ono’s book of art instructions, Grapefruit. The archive edition includes recordings from before the final arrangement. [Lennon’s account and single history](https://www.johnlennon.com/music/singles/imagine/) · [2018 edition notes][imagine]
2. **Crippled Inside.** Harrison plays Dobro. The archive edition includes Take 6 with an alternate solo. [2018 edition notes][imagine]
3. **Jealous Guy.** Badfinger guitarists Joey Molland and Tom Evans played on the song from Take 9 onward. [Personnel and Take 9 notes][imagine]
4. **It's So Hard.** The session tape captures Lennon singing an example riff for saxophonist King Curtis. The exchange appears in the Evolution Documentary. [Evolution Documentary notes][imagine]
5. **I Don't Wanna Be a Soldier Mama, I Don't Wanna Die.** The February recording, Take 11, has a looser, reggae-influenced feel and features Bobby Keys on saxophone. King Curtis plays on the album version. [2018 edition notes][imagine]
6. **Gimme Some Truth.** Take 4 documents the song before the guitar solo and electric harpsichord were added. [2018 edition notes][imagine]
7. **Oh My Love.** Alan White played Tibetan cymbals. The archive also includes a photo of Ono and Phil Spector in the control room on May 28, 1971. [Personnel and session photos][imagine]
8. **How Do You Sleep?** Lennon said he heard jabs in McCartney’s Ram. He later described the song as also directed at himself. Harrison played guitar on the recording. [Lennon interviews, including Cash Box, December 11, 1971][cashbox] · [Evolution Documentary notes][imagine] · [Archive credits](https://www.johnlennon.com/music/rarities/anthology/)
9. **How?** Take 31 includes John Tout on vibraphone. Strings replaced that part on the album. [2018 edition notes][imagine]
10. **Oh Yoko!** The archive includes Lennon and Ono singing part of the song for Derek Taylor in the Bahamas in 1969. Spector sang harmony on the 1971 album recording. [2018 edition notes][imagine]

In a 2018 interview, McCartney described “Dear Friend” from *Wild Life* as a way to reach out to Lennon after the disputes around the Beatles’ breakup. [McCartney’s Wild Life interview](https://www.paulmccartney.com/news/you-gave-me-the-answer-wild-life-special)

## McCartney

*Paul McCartney · 13 tracks*

McCartney began recording at home on a four-track machine and played the instruments himself. Linda McCartney sang harmonies and photographed the cherries on the front cover in Antigua and Paul with their daughter Mary for the back cover. [Album notes][mac] · [Cover photography][mac50]

1. **The Lovely Linda.** McCartney used the short opener to test his new home recording equipment. [McCartney’s 1970 press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]
2. **That Would Be Something.** McCartney wrote the song in Scotland and played acoustic guitar, tom-toms, cymbals, and bass on the recording. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]
3. **Valentine Day.** McCartney composed the parts as he recorded, layering guitar, drums, and bass. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]
4. **Every Night.** McCartney’s press notes trace the song back to a vacation in Greece. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]
5. **Hot as Sun / Glasses.** Overdubbed wine glasses create the ringing sound in “Glasses.” A snippet of “Suicide” appears at the end, and a longer version was issued later in the Archive Collection. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp] · [Archive Collection][mac11]
6. **Junk.** McCartney recorded an Esher demo during the Beatles era. It was released on the White Album anniversary edition. [Esher demos tracklist](https://www.thebeatles.com/beatles-white-album-tracklisting) · [Solo album archive][mac]
7. **Man We Was Lonely.** McCartney used a drum tuning key on a Telecaster to produce a sound resembling a steel guitar. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]
8. **Oo You.** McCartney lists an aerosol spray alongside the cowbell and tambourine among the sounds on the track. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]
9. **Momma Miss America.** The instrumental grew out of an improvisation at home. McCartney recorded the piano, drums, and guitar. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]
10. **Teddy Boy.** The Beatles tried the song in January 1969. McCartney finished his solo version at Morgan Studios. [Anthology session notes][anth] · [LP archive][maclp]
11. **Singalong Junk.** McCartney kept an early backing track for “Junk” as an instrumental and added instruments including Mellotron. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]
12. **Maybe I'm Amazed.** The promotional video uses family photographs of Paul, Linda, and Heather. The studio version appeared on the 1970 album, and a live recording by Wings later became a single. [LP archive][maclp] · [McCartney’s release archive](https://www.paulmccartney.com/timeline/1970)
13. **Kreen-Akrore.** A television documentary about an Indigenous people in Brazil inspired McCartney. He started with drums and added breathing sounds and bow-and-arrow effects. [McCartney’s press notes][macqa] · [LP archive][maclp]

## Living in the Material World

*George Harrison · 11 tracks*

Harrison donated publishing royalties from the album’s songs to the Material World Foundation, which continued his charitable work following the *Concert for Bangladesh*. The packaging includes imagery from the *Bhagavad-Gita*. [50th anniversary notes][georgenews] · [Album packaging](https://www.georgeharrison.com/releases/living-in-the-material-world/)

1. **Give Me Love (Give Me Peace on Earth).** The single and the album both topped the US charts. Acoustic Take 18 offers a sparser arrangement. [50th anniversary notes][georgenews] · [2024 session tracklist][george]
2. **Sue Me, Sue You Blues.** The 2006 edition included the acoustic demo. The 2024 edition adds Take 5 for another comparison with the album recording. [Acoustic demo video](https://www.georgeharrison.com/videos/sue-me-sue-you-blues-acoustic-demo/) · [2024 session tracklist][george]
3. **The Light That Has Lighted the World.** Harrison recalled starting the song as a possible B-side for Cilla Black. He thought about their experiences leaving Liverpool and the reactions of people back home. [Harrison’s account in I Me Mine, p. 268][me]
4. **Don't Let Me Wait Too Long.** The 2024 edition includes the acoustic Take 49. [2024 session tracklist][george]
5. **Who Can See It.** The 2024 edition identifies the alternate recording as Take 93. [2024 session tracklist][george]
6. **Living in the Material World.** Take 31 of the title track was issued on the 2024 edition. The song shares its name with Harrison’s charitable foundation. [2024 session tracklist][george] · [Foundation history][georgenews]
7. **The Lord Loves the One (That Loves the Lord).** Harrison recalled writing the song after a visit and conversation with Swami A. C. Bhaktivedanta. [Harrison’s account in I Me Mine][me] · [2024 session tracklist][george]
8. **Be Here Now.** Take 8 was recorded October 12, 1972. Harrison’s estate later released it with a new animation. [Official Take 8 release](https://www.georgeharrison.com/be-here-now/)
9. **Try Some Buy Some.** Ronnie Spector recorded Harrison’s composition first. David Bowie later covered it on Reality. [Harrison archive on the Spector and Bowie recordings](https://www.georgeharrison.com/bowie-covers-george/)
10. **The Day the World Gets 'Round.** Harrison recalled beginning the song in New York the day after the Concert for Bangladesh. He remembered being moved by the volunteers and frustrated by the scale of the problems governments had yet to address. [Harrison’s account in I Me Mine, p. 226][me] · [2024 acoustic version][george]
11. **That Is All.** Take 24 is the alternate recording of the album closer on the 2024 edition. [2024 session tracklist][george]

## Ringo

*Ringo Starr · 10 tracks*

Lennon, McCartney, and Harrison contributed to different tracks. Starr later saw the record as a forerunner to the way he worked with his All-Starr Band. [Starr interview](https://www.goldminemag.com/interviews/a-conversation-with-ringo-starr-on-his-83rd-birthday/) · [LP credits][ringo]

1. **I'm the Greatest.** Lennon plays piano and sings, Harrison plays guitar, and Starr drums and sings. Billy Preston and Klaus Voormann also appear on the track. [LP credits][ringo] · [Session archive](https://www.jpgr.co.uk/pctc252.html)
2. **Have You Seen My Baby.** Marc Bolan of T. Rex plays guitar on this Randy Newman song. [LP credits][ringo]
3. **Photograph.** Harrison co-wrote the song with Starr and contributed 12-string guitar and harmony vocals. Bobby Keys played tenor saxophone. [LP credits][ringo]
4. **Sunshine Life for Me (Sail Away Raymond).** Several members of The Band accompany Starr in an arrangement that includes mandolin and fiddle. Harrison’s estate released an alternate version in 2024. [LP credits][ringo] · [Alternate recording](https://www.georgeharrison.com/sunshin-life-for-me/)
5. **You're Sixteen (You're Beautiful and You're Mine).** McCartney is credited with imitating a saxophone with his voice. Harry Nilsson also sings on the recording. [LP credits][ringo]
6. **Oh My My.** Martha Reeves and Merry Clayton sing backing vocals. Billy Preston plays keyboards. [LP credits][ringo]
7. **Step Lightly.** Starr’s tap dancing was recorded at Apple Studios on April 16. [Session archive and LP credits](https://www.jpgr.co.uk/pctc252.html)
8. **Six O'Clock.** McCartney plays piano and synthesizer and sings harmonies with Linda. Both are credited as writers. [LP credits][ringo] · [Label’s digital credits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZZH_z60fkhg)
9. **Devil Woman.** The recording credits list Starr and Jim Keltner on drums. Starr co-wrote the song with Vini Poncia. [LP credits][ringo] · [Label’s songwriting credits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1ICQJTLy31I)
10. **You and Me (Babe).** Harrison and Mal Evans wrote the closing track. Milt Holland plays marimba. [LP credits][ringo] · [Label’s songwriting credits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0gw2_li3i4U)

## How these notes appear in Discora

- Track stories follow the songwriter credits and writing notes. Album stories follow the tracklist and release notes, with a shortcut near the collection button.
- Album and artist pages include photo galleries, with additional galleries on selected track pages. Controls support swipe and button navigation. The slideshow advances when visible and honors reduced-motion preferences.
- Future research can revisit tracks currently covered through alternate takes, especially on *Living in the Material World*, and check reuse rights for original studio photographs. Accounts of disagreements should identify the speaker and when the statement was made.
